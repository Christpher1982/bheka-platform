# Product vision

Status note: the market and ideal-customer-profile sections below are Provisional pending
the 15 structured discovery interviews (five BPO operations directors, five bank/insurer
CISOs, five Information Officers) referenced throughout this blueprint. Everything here is
the current best reasoning from public sources, not a validated go-to-market plan.

## 1. The problem

South African organisations have a worsening insider-risk problem and no lawful,
domestically-built tool to answer it. Forty-six percent of South African organisations
report rising malicious insider incidents, up from 33% in 2024
([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/employee-vengeance-a-major-concern-as-internal-security-incidents-increase/Gb3Bw7WaxAbq2k6V)).
Independent reporting puts a data breach in South Africa at roughly one every three
hours, with about 90% assessed as preventable and around 284 notifications a month
([IOL](https://iol.co.za/business-report/economy/2026-03-20-data-breaches-in-south-africa-occur-every-three-hours-with-90-deemed-preventable/)).
Globally, IBM's 2025 breach research finds malicious insiders the single costliest attack
vector at USD 4.92 million per breach
([summary of IBM findings](https://www.bluefin.com/bluefin-news/ibms-2025-data-breach-report-key-findings-and-the-years-biggest-attacks/)),
and the 2026 Ponemon/DTEX cost-of-insider-risk study puts the average organisational cost
at USD 19.5 million a year, 67 days to contain an incident, and USD 8.2 million saved by
organisations running a formal insider-risk programme
([Ponemon/DTEX](https://ponemon.dtex.ai/)).

Every credible tool available to a South African buyer today is foreign, hosted outside
Africa, and built around an architecture — record everything, often covertly — that is
difficult to operate lawfully under South African labour and privacy law. RICA section 6
permits business-purpose interception only where the controller "has made all reasonable
efforts to inform in advance" the people using the system
([PPM Attorneys](https://www.ppmattorneys.co.za/can-employers-monitor-employees-activities/)).
Teramind's flagship differentiator is a stealth agent with tamper protection gated behind
it ([Teramind KB](https://kb.teramind.co/en/articles/8790994-what-is-the-difference-between-the-revealed-agent-and-the-hidden-agent)).
Insightful and Syteca also ship covert modes
(see `Teramind_assessment_and_build_thesis_2026-07-31.md` for the full sourced
comparison). None of the major vendors hold African data residency
([Teramind AWS deployment KB](https://kb.teramind.co/en/articles/9227127-aws-deployment)).
The market leaders' most-promoted capability is frequently the one a South African
buyer's own lawyer will strike from the contract.

## 2. The market

Insider risk management is estimated at USD 7.09 billion in 2026, growing 12.32% a year
([Data Library Research](https://www.datalibraryresearch.com/reports/insider-risk-management-market-3539)),
and the adjacent DLP market at USD 42.87 billion, growing 21.17%
([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/data-loss-prevention-market)).
The Middle East and Africa slice of employee-monitoring software specifically is small —
USD 56 million in 2026
([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/industry-reports/employee-surveillance-and-monitoring-software-market-104796)) —
which describes an unserved market rather than an absent one, given the incident
statistics above.

Regulation has just turned into a buying trigger rather than a compliance afterthought.
The Information Regulator told Parliament in May 2026 that it is moving to "structured,
proactive compliance monitoring" with sector sweeps across insurance, banking, telecoms,
retail, education and government
([People of Internet](https://peopleofinternet.com/articles/south-africa-s-popia-regulator-pivots-from-complaint-handler.html)).
Enforcement is real, if still modest in scale: the Information Regulator has issued R5
million infringement notices against the Department of Justice and Constitutional
Development (2023, for failing to renew security software licences after a ransomware
attack) and the Department of Basic Education (2024, later set aside on appeal), plus
R500,000 against Blouberg Municipality and R100,000 each against FT Rams Consulting and
Lancet Laboratories
([Werksmans summary of the 2025/26 Annual Performance Plan](https://werksmans.com/south-africas-information-regulator-what-the-2025-26-annual-performance-plan-means-for-business-as-presented-to-the-portfolio-committee-on-5-may-2026/);
[Bowmans on the DoJ&CD fine](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/south-africa-beware-information-regulator-issues-first-fine-of-zar-5-million-under-popia/)).
Reported data breach notifications ran at roughly 2,374 in 2024/25 and 1,947 so far in
2025, a 40% year-on-year increase
([SEESA](https://www.seesa.co.za/blog/information-regulator-gives-update-on-popia-paia-matters/)).

Separately, Joint Standard 2 of 2024 on cybersecurity and cyber resilience, issued by the
Financial Sector Conduct Authority and Prudential Authority, commenced 1 June 2025
([Joint Notice 1 of 2024](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/prudential-authority/pa-public-awareness/covid-19-response/2024/joint-comms-5-of-2024/Joint%20Notice%201%20of%202024%20-%20Commencement%20-%20Cybersecurity%20Joint%20Standard.pdf)),
giving every bank, insurer, financial services provider and pension fund in the country a
board-level obligation and, typically, a budget line.

## 3. The African software sovereignty thesis

The thesis is not "build Teramind, but South African." It is that South Africa's legal
and regulatory environment makes a class of product architecture that incumbents cannot
easily retrofit: consent-first by design, data resident in South Africa, ZAR-priced, with
compliance artefacts as a first-class output rather than something the customer's legal
team assembles after the fact.

Three structural facts support this:

1. **Data residency is a hard requirement for the government buyer, not a preference.**
   The DPSA cloud computing directive states that government data must reside within
   South Africa, or the provider must comply with POPIA section 72 cross-border transfer
   safeguards
   ([Michalsons summary of the DPSA directive](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/directive-on-cloud-computing-in-the-public-service-dpsa/55782)).
   The 2024 National Policy on Data and Cloud goes further for national-security-related
   data, requiring storage "only in digital infrastructure located within the borders of
   South Africa," with penalties reaching 10% of global turnover
   ([ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2025/06/09/south-africa-localization-regulation/)).
2. **The public-sector procurement door is structurally closed to foreign vendors.** SITA
   product certification is OEM-only, explicitly excludes resellers, requires a signed
   Memorandum of Agreement, and states "no sales of uncertified products to Government
   will be allowed"
   ([SITA Technical Certification Process](https://www.sita.co.za/sites/default/files/documents/Product_Certification/Tech_Certification_Process_(TCP).pdf)).
   A foreign vendor selling through a local reseller cannot walk through this door. A
   South African-built OEM can.
3. **There is no genuinely African-built product in this category today.** Sizasana reads
   as a white-label of another vendor's engine
   ([Sizasana](https://sizasana.co.za/monitoring/)), TTS resells Microsoft Purview
   priced in rand ([TTS](https://ttscyber.co.za/products/insider-risk-management-service)),
   and J2 Software distributes DTEX
   ([J2](https://www.j2software.co.uk/j2-blog/159-10-years-in-south-africa-where-are-we-now)).
   The local market is entirely intermediated and margin leaves the country with every
   sale.

Sovereignty here is not a nationalist slogan; it is three concrete, verifiable facts: the
root keys are held in-region or by the customer (`CANON.md` section 6), the data does not
leave the continent unless the customer opts in, and the compliance evidence a South
African Information Officer must produce is generated by the product rather than
assembled by hand.

## 4. Ideal customer profile

Provisional — this profile is the working hypothesis pending the 15 discovery interviews,
not a validated segmentation.

**Primary beachhead: BPO and contact centre operators serving UK/US clients.**
South Africa's business process outsourcing sector serves clients that impose contractual
monitoring obligations the local operator cannot negotiate away. These buyers already
purchase foreign monitoring tools, already carry a compliance requirement handed down by
their own client, and typically run hundreds of seats per site — high seat count, clear
budget owner, and comparatively low internal political resistance because the monitoring
mandate originates externally.

**Secondary: regulated financial services (banks, insurers, FSPs, pension funds).**
Joint Standard 2 of 2024 gives a named reason to call, and the sale is the compliance
evidence pack — RICA notification registers, POPIA minimality justifications, breach
notification drafts, Joint Standard control mappings — not the surveillance capability
itself.

**Tertiary: public sector and state-owned entities, gated on SITA OEM certification.**
Slower and more political, but structurally protected from foreign competition once
certified, and large in aggregate seat count.

**Not a target in v1:** organisations whose primary interest is covert monitoring,
BYOD monitoring, or productivity surveillance detached from a defensible risk trigger.
These requests are refused per `CANON.md` section 5 and `000_PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md`
section 2, and pursuing them would contradict the product's legal and brand foundation.

**Against Teramind, Veriato, Syteca:** the wedge is architectural, not cosmetic. These
products' most-promoted capability — covert, always-on capture — is the one a South
African employer's own labour lawyer is most likely to strike from a deployment, and none
hold African data residency.

**Against DTEX:** DTEX is the strategically closest peer and the proof of concept for the
underlying thesis. It raised USD 138 million including a USD 50 million Series E led by
Alphabet's CapitalG on a metadata-first, no-screen-recording model
([DTEX press release](https://www.dtexsystems.com/newsroom/press-releases/dtex-systems-raises-50m-led-by-alphabets-capitalg-to-disrupt-insider-risk-management-market-through-ai-enabled-innovations/)),
and holds a full FedRAMP ATO
([DTEX](https://www.dtex.ai/blog/dtex-cnssd-504-fedramp-marketplace/)). DTEX validates
that recording less can win federal-grade trust; it does not serve South Africa directly
and holds no African data residency, SITA certification, or POPIA/RICA compliance-artefact
generation.

**Against ActivTrak:** ActivTrak is the closest match on privacy posture — no keystroke
logging, no covert mode, screenshots off by default
([ActivTrak features](https://www.activtrak.com/features/)) — but it is a workforce
analytics product, not an insider-risk and DLP platform, has no South African data
residency, and does not generate POPIA, RICA, or Joint Standard compliance artefacts.
Bheka's Tier 1 baseline is deliberately positioned to be ActivTrak-comparable in
intrusiveness while adding insider-risk detection and a compliance module ActivTrak does
not attempt.

**Against Microsoft Purview Insider Risk Management:** Purview IRM bundled into M365 E5
is priced low enough (E5 Compliance around USD 144/user/year against Teramind DLP at
roughly USD 420/user/year,
[Microsoft Negotiations](https://microsoftnegotiations.com/blog/purview-insider-risk-management-licensing))
that any new entrant competing on generic insider-risk feature parity loses on price
before the first demo. Bheka does not compete on price against Microsoft. It competes on
data residency Microsoft does not offer specifically for this workload, forensic session
evidence Purview does not produce, South African compliance artefacts Purview does not
generate, and SITA OEM eligibility that is structurally unavailable to Microsoft as a
foreign hyperscaler product.

## 6. What Bheka is not trying to be

Bheka is not a general workforce-analytics or time-tracking tool (that space is served,
imperfectly, by ActivTrak, Hubstaff and Insightful — see
`ActivTrak_and_alternatives_2026-07-31.md`), and it will not compete on raw feature count
against Teramind. The bet is narrower and, we believe, more durable: be the only insider-
risk platform a South African compliance officer, labour lawyer, and CISO can all sign off
on in the same meeting.
