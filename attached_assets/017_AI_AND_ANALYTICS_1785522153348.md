# AI and analytics

Status note: the specific model architectures, thresholds, and bias-testing cadence below
are Provisional pending attorney review of the POPIA section 71 analysis and pending the
first live tenant's bias-audit baseline. The hard constraint in section 1 is Locked and
must not be weakened by any future model change without a board-approved ADR, per
`000_PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md` section 2.

## 1. The hard constraint (Locked, non-negotiable)

**No model output alone may trigger an adverse employment decision.** Bheka's risk
scores, anomaly flags, and investigation copilot outputs are inputs to a human
investigation. They are never, under any customer configuration, wired directly to a
disciplinary action, termination, account suspension used as discipline, or any other
consequence for a named individual without a documented human decision in between. This
is one of the eight permanent refusals in `CANON.md` section 5 ("Automated adverse
employment decisions. Bheka produces evidence; humans decide.") and it is enforced at the
product level, not left to customer policy:

- There is no API endpoint, webhook, or integration pattern in `bheka-policy` or
  `bheka-case` that allows a detection or risk score to directly call an HRIS
  termination, suspension, or disciplinary-record-creation action. Any HRIS integration
  is read-only for joiner-mover-leaver signal ingestion, and any writeback is limited to
  case status, never to an employment action.
- Every case that could lead to a disciplinary outcome must pass through
  `bheka-case` with an assigned human investigator, a documented rationale, and (for
  Tier 3 evidence) the dual-authorisation and audit trail defined in `CANON.md` section 4.
- The investigation copilot (section 4 below) drafts summaries and suggests lines of
  inquiry. It does not and will not have a "recommend termination" or "confidence score
  for dismissal" output. Its role stops at organizing evidence for a human to judge.

This constraint exists for two independent reasons that both have to hold. First, it is
the product's contractual and marketing position; violating it once destroys the trust
basis the entire compliance-first positioning depends on (`001_PRODUCT_VISION.md` section
6). Second, it is very likely a POPIA section 71 obligation, not just a preference — see
section 6 below.

## 2. Risk scoring

Bheka's risk score is a per-user, explainable composite of behavioural signals collected
under the tiered visibility model (`CANON.md` section 4). Score computation and storage
happen in `bheka-policy` and are written to the `risk_scores` table
(see `schemas/database/*.sql` — prose here must not restate the schema, per the anti-drift
rule in `CANON.md` section 14).

Design commitments:

- **Every score decomposes into the named signals that produced it.** There is no
  opaque single number with no explanation. This is not a nice-to-have; it is close to a
  CCMA evidentiary requirement in practice — evidence that cannot be explained is weaker
  evidence at arbitration (see `019_LEGAL_AND_REGULATORY_FRAMEWORK.md` section 6 on
  admissibility).
- **Peer-group baselining, not absolute thresholds**, by role, team and department, so
  that a role with inherently high file-access volume (finance, legal, engineering) is
  not flagged for behaviour that is normal for that role.
- **30-day rolling baseline per user**, recalculated continuously, so a score reflects
  recent behaviour rather than a stale historical snapshot.
- **No sentiment, emotion, or "loyalty" input of any kind**, per the permanent refusal in
  `CANON.md` section 5. Flight-risk and resignation-precursor indicators are built from
  observable actions (mass file access, CV-site visits, bulk downloads before notice), not
  from inferred emotional state.
- **Score changes do not silently change tier.** A score crossing a threshold can trigger
  a Tier 2 escalation (bounded, logged, per `CANON.md` section 4), but Tier 3 always
  requires the separate dual-authorisation flow regardless of score.

## 3. Anomaly detection

Anomaly detection runs identically across every deployment model — cloud, private cloud,
and on-premise. This is a deliberate point of difference: Teramind restricts
anomaly-based rules to on-premise deployments, meaning the cloud product most buyers
actually use has materially weaker behavioural detection than the version almost nobody
deploys
([Teramind risk-scoring knowledge base](https://kb.teramind.co/en/articles/8790922-how-does-teramind-define-risk)).
Bheka's anomaly models are packaged and versioned the same way regardless of where the
customer's tenant runs.

Detected patterns include, per the feature specification
(`Eride_security_product_feature_spec_2026-07-31.md`, Module D): per-user behavioural
deviation from peer baseline, sequence detection across a kill chain (for example, large
query, local copy, USB insert, upload attempt treated as one connected pattern rather than
four isolated alerts), dormant and orphaned account activity, privileged-user monitoring
with a separate tighter baseline, shared-account and credential-sharing detection via
typing rhythm and device fingerprint, and impossible-travel or concurrent-session
detection.

Anomaly detection content — the rules, models, and thresholds — is treated as versioned,
schema-validated, and ring-deployed exactly like agent binaries, per the CrowdStrike
Channel File 291 lesson documented in `Eride_build_and_distribution_plan_2026-07-31.md`
Part 5 (the root cause analysis is at
[CrowdStrike's own RCA](https://www.crowdstrike.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Channel-File-291-Incident-Root-Cause-Analysis-08.06.2024.pdf)).
Detection content is code: it is canaried, ring-deployed, and rollback-capable, because a
bad detection update that floods every tenant with false positives (or silently stops
detecting) is a trust-ending failure in this category, not a minor bug.

## 4. The investigation copilot

The copilot is a generative-AI assistant inside `bheka-case` that helps a human
investigator work faster through an already-open case. It is scoped narrowly:

- **Input**: evidence already collected and stored under an open case record (timeline
  events, detection triggers, prior investigator notes). The copilot does not initiate
  new data collection and cannot itself escalate a tier.
- **Output**: a draft timeline narrative, a suggested list of follow-up questions or
  evidence gaps, and plain-language summaries of technical signals (for example,
  translating a sequence of file-system events into a readable account of what happened)
  for the human investigator to review, edit, and take ownership of.
- **What it will never output**: a recommendation to terminate, discipline, or take any
  named adverse action against an individual; a probability or confidence score framed as
  "likelihood this person is guilty"; or any similar framing that would function as an
  automated adverse decision by another name. This is enforced at the prompt and output
  schema level, not left to model behaviour, and any output resembling a disciplinary
  recommendation is treated as a defect to be fixed, not a feature to be refined.
- **Every copilot-assisted draft is attributed and logged.** The `audit_log` records that
  AI assistance was used in preparing a case artefact, so a court-admissible export pack
  (Module E of the feature specification) discloses AI involvement rather than presenting
  a machine-drafted summary as purely human analysis.
- **Pseudonymity is preserved through the copilot.** Where the case is still in
  pseudonymous mode (analysts seeing `USER-4471`, not a name), the copilot operates on the
  pseudonymised record and does not re-identify the subject as a side effect of
  summarisation.

## 5. Model governance

Provisional — the specific governance cadence below is a reasonable default, not yet
tested against a live tenant or confirmed by external counsel.

- **Model inventory.** Every model or rule set in production (risk scoring, anomaly
  detection, the investigation copilot's underlying LLM configuration) is registered with
  a named owner, a version, a training/tuning data description, and a last-reviewed date.
- **Change control.** A new model version cannot go to production without passing the
  bias-testing gate in section 6, a documented review of any change to what data it
  consumes, and a ring-based rollout consistent with the update-ring model in `CANON.md`
  section 11.
- **Explainability requirement.** Any model whose output can influence a case (risk
  scoring, anomaly flags) must produce a decomposition a human investigator can read
  without machine-learning expertise. Models that cannot meet this bar do not ship,
  regardless of accuracy gains, because unexplainable evidence is weak evidence in this
  product's actual use case (an employment dispute or a criminal referral).
- **No training on customer data across tenants.** Per-tenant models or tenant-isolated
  feature stores only; Bheka does not pool customer telemetry to train a shared model that
  crosses tenant boundaries, consistent with the per-tenant key custody model in
  `CANON.md` section 6 and the stated refusal to sell, share, or train third-party models
  on customer telemetry (feature specification, section 4).
- **Human override is always available and logged.** Any automated flag can be dismissed,
  downgraded, or escalated by a human investigator, and that action is itself an audit
  event.

## 6. Bias testing

Provisional — pending confirmation of the specific fairness metrics and thresholds with
external counsel and, ideally, an external AI-audit partner before the first tenant goes
live with risk scoring enabled.

Bheka's risk scores and anomaly flags are not a hiring or credit decision, but they can
influence which employees get investigated, and unequal false-positive rates across
groups would be both an ethical failure and a POPIA and labour-law exposure. The
methodology to adopt, informed by the general literature on algorithmic fairness in
employment-adjacent systems:

- **Pre-deployment fairness testing** against protected characteristics available in
  HRIS data (role, department, tenure, and, where lawfully collected, demographic
  categories), using standard fairness metrics such as demographic parity difference,
  equalised odds difference, and disparate impact ratio, with a documented pass/fail
  threshold before a model version ships — a pattern increasingly standard in employment-
  adjacent AI auditing
  ([overview of automated bias-testing gates](https://www.systemshardening.com/articles/ai-landscape/algorithmic-auditing/)).
  A commonly used disparate-impact threshold in employment contexts is the four-fifths
  (0.8) rule derived from US Uniform Guidelines practice; Bheka treats this as a useful
  reference point, not a South African legal standard, pending attorney confirmation of
  what a South African court or the CCMA would consider an appropriate fairness bar.
- **Continuous post-deployment monitoring**, not a one-time test, because bias can emerge
  after deployment as the tenant's workforce composition or work patterns shift.
- **Blocking failures halt deployment.** A model version that fails a configured fairness
  threshold does not ship to production regardless of accuracy improvements, and this is
  enforced in CI, not left to reviewer discretion.
- **Documented remediation, not silent exclusion.** If a bias audit finds a disparity,
  the fix (reweighting, feature removal, threshold adjustment) is documented, and the
  before/after fairness metrics are retained as part of the model's version history for
  audit purposes.
- **External audit before any Tier B or Tier C customer (CANON section 6) goes live with
  risk scoring on a workforce above a size threshold to be set with counsel.** Banks and
  regulated financial institutions in particular will expect evidence of bias testing as
  part of Joint Standard 2 of 2024 due diligence.

## 7. POPIA section 71 (automated decision making) and how it applies

POPIA section 71(1) provides that a data subject may not be subject to a decision which
results in legal consequences for them, or which affects them to a substantial degree,
based solely on automated processing of personal information intended to profile them —
including their performance at work, reliability, or conduct
([popia.co.za, section 71 text](https://popia.co.za/section-71-automated-decision-making/)).
Section 71(3) requires that, where an exemption applies, the responsible party must
provide the data subject with "sufficient information about the underlying logic of the
automated processing... to enable him or her to make representations" — a transparency
duty distinct from, but related to, a right of notification
([De Rebus analysis of section 71](https://www.derebus.org.za/has-popia-adequately-prepared-people-to-exercise-their-right-not-to-be-subject-to-automated-decision-making/)).
More recent practitioner analysis frames section 71 as a request right the data subject
must invoke, requiring the responsible party to have a procedure for genuine human review
— not "rubber-stamp" sign-off — with access to the full decision record
([TenetAI's practitioner summary](https://tenetai.dev/blog/south-africa-popia-ai-compliance)).

How this maps onto Bheka, marked Provisional pending attorney sign-off on the specific
legal conclusion:

- **Bheka's risk scores and anomaly flags are very likely "profiling" within the meaning
  of section 71(1)** — they process personal information to produce a profile bearing on
  conduct, reliability, and performance at work. Practitioner guidance treats
  workplace-conduct profiling of this kind as squarely within section 71's scope
  ([Juta practitioner note on vetting and screening under POPIA](https://cms.juta.co.za/documents/45/Issue_no_5_Vetting_and_Screening_Data_Subjects_Under_POPIA.pdf)).
- **Because the hard constraint in section 1 ensures no Bheka output alone produces the
  "decision"** — a human investigator always makes the actual disciplinary or employment
  call, informed by the score but not bound by it — Bheka's architecture is designed to
  sit outside the narrowest reading of section 71's prohibition, which applies to
  decisions based "solely" on automated processing. This is the product's legal theory,
  not yet a confirmed legal conclusion, and it is precisely why the hard constraint in
  section 1 cannot be weakened without board and legal sign-off.
- **The transparency obligation still applies to the scoring itself.** Even where a human
  makes the final call, Bheka's explainable-score design (section 2) exists partly to
  satisfy a section 71(3)-style "sufficient information about the underlying logic"
  standard if a data subject ever contests a decision that was informed by a Bheka score.
- **Open question for counsel:** whether an employee has a right to request that a
  specific Bheka-informed decision not be based on automated profiling under section
  71(1), and what operational process (beyond the existing human-in-the-loop
  architecture) that would require. This is marked Open pending attorney review — see
  `019_LEGAL_AND_REGULATORY_FRAMEWORK.md` section 2.

## AI implementation constraints

- No code path may connect a `risk_scores` or `detections` record directly to an
  HRIS write action that constitutes or triggers a disciplinary or termination action.
  This must be enforced by API design (no such endpoint exists), not by policy alone.
- The investigation copilot's system prompt and output schema must not include fields for
  disciplinary recommendation, guilt probability, or termination likelihood. Any such
  field discovered in a prompt template or output schema is a release-blocking defect.
- Bias-testing gates must run in CI for every model version before production deployment,
  per section 6, with blocking failures preventing merge, not just logging a warning.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a risk score or anomaly detection is generated, when it is displayed to an
  investigator, then it must show a decomposition of the contributing signals, not a
  single opaque number.
- Given a new model version is proposed for production, when it is submitted for release,
  then it must pass the configured bias-testing thresholds in CI or be blocked from
  deployment.
- Given a case reaches a point where disciplinary action is being considered, when the
  case record is reviewed, then it must show a documented human investigator decision
  distinct from any automated score or flag, with no direct system linkage from score to
  employment action.
- Given the investigation copilot drafts a case summary, when that summary is included in
  an evidence export, then the export must disclose that AI assistance was used.
