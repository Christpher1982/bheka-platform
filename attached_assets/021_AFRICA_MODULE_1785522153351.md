# Africa module

Status note: the South African operating-conditions requirements in section 1 are Locked
per `CANON.md` section 16. The continental expansion analysis in sections 2–4 is
Provisional — it is desk research to inform sequencing, not a validated go-to-market or
legal-entry plan for any specific country.

## 1. South African operating conditions (Locked, CANON section 16)

Bheka's agent and platform are built for South African infrastructure conditions, not
retrofitted from a US/EU-first design. This is a Locked requirement, not an aspiration.

**Load-shedding resilience.** The agent uses journaled writes so that an abrupt power
loss does not corrupt the local buffer, and telemetry survives ungraceful shutdown. This
is a basic reliability requirement in a country where scheduled and unscheduled power
outages are routine, not an edge case to be handled opportunistically.

**Offline buffering with backpressure.** Up to 30 days of telemetry is buffered locally
in SQLCipher-encrypted storage when the agent cannot reach `bheka-ingest`, with disk-quota
enforcement so a prolonged outage cannot fill an endpoint's disk. Backpressure logic
governs what is dropped or summarised first if the buffer approaches capacity (metadata
before content, oldest before newest), and this policy must be documented and testable,
not implicit.

**Low-bandwidth mode.** Aggressive batching, zstd compression, and a metadata-only
fallback mode for constrained links, so that a branch office on a poor ADSL or LTE
connection does not either flood the link or silently stop reporting.

**Multi-language transparency notices.** Employee-facing notices are delivered in
English, isiZulu, Afrikaans, Sesotho, and isiXhosa, reflecting South Africa's most widely
spoken languages, and reinforcing the RICA section 6 "reasonable efforts to inform in
advance" standard discussed in `019_LEGAL_AND_REGULATORY_FRAMEWORK.md` section 3 — a
notice only an English-speaking employee can read does not meet that standard for a
multilingual workforce.

**WhatsApp Business API for administrator alerting.** Given WhatsApp's dominant position
as a business communication channel in South Africa, critical alerts (Tier 3 activation
requests, breach-relevant detections, agent health issues) can be routed to administrators
via WhatsApp Business API in addition to email and console notifications, rather than
assuming an administrator is watching an email inbox or dashboard continuously.

**ZAR billing and local payment rails.** Bheka is priced and billed in South African rand
by default, with no forced conversion to or billing in US dollars, and supports local
payment rails rather than requiring an international card or wire transfer. See
`027_PRICING_AND_PACKAGING_COMMERCIAL.md` for the specific pricing tiers and billing
mechanics.

## 2. Why expand beyond South Africa

The thesis in `001_PRODUCT_VISION.md` section 3 — that data residency law and public-
sector procurement structure create defensible ground for a domestically-built vendor —
is not unique to South Africa. Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana each have comparable data
protection regimes with a registration or licensing requirement that functions similarly
to South Africa's Information Regulator model, and each represents a market where a
foreign vendor faces the same structural disadvantages Bheka is designed to exploit
domestically. This section is Provisional — it establishes that a legal hook exists in
each market, not that Bheka has validated demand, pricing, or a go-to-market motion there.

## 3. Nigeria — Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023

Full text: [NITDA/NDPC gazette copy](https://cert.gov.ng/ngcert/resources/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf);
official download: [Nigeria Data Protection Commission](https://ndpc.gov.ng/download/nigeria-data-protection-act-2023).
The NDPA establishes the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) as the supervisory
authority and introduces a compliance-audit and registration regime for data controllers
and processors of a certain size, with the Commission empowered to issue further
regulations. A detailed review of the Act's structure and obligations is available via
[G. Elias & Co.'s legal review](https://www.gelias.com/images/Newsletter/The_Nigerian_Data_Protection_Act_2023_-_A_Review.pdf).
Relevance to Bheka: Nigeria's BPO and financial services sectors are large and would be a
natural extension of the same beachhead logic used domestically (`001_PRODUCT_VISION.md`
section 4), but the NDPC's registration and compliance-audit mechanics have not yet been
mapped against Bheka's product controls the way `020_POPIA_CONTROL_MAP.md` does for South
Africa. This is Open.

## 4. Kenya — Data Protection Act, 2019

The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) is the supervisory authority
([ODPC official site](https://www.odpc.go.ke/)), operating under the mandate of the Data
Protection Act 2019, and maintains an online registration portal for data controllers and
processors
([ODPC registration portal](https://dataportal.odpc.go.ke/Account/Register)). Kenya's
Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021
provide a turnover-based exemption from registration for entities below a threshold
(reported elsewhere in this project's research as roughly KES 5 million annual turnover
for non-sensitive processing categories), though this exemption detail has not been
independently re-verified against the Regulations' primary text for this document and
should be treated as Open pending confirmation. Kenya also restricts cross-border data
transfers more assertively than South Africa in some respects
([DLA Piper's Data Protection Laws of the World, Kenya entry](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=KE)),
which would need specific analysis before Bheka's default af-south-1 hosting model could
be assumed adequate for a Kenyan deployment.

## 5. Ghana — Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843)

The Data Protection Commission (DPC) requires data controllers to register before
processing personal data:  section 46–47 establish the register, section 27(1) requires a
written application in the prescribed form, section 53 prohibits processing personal data
without being registered (subject to listed exceptions), and section 56 sets penalties for
contravention at a fine of up to 250 penalty units, imprisonment of up to two years, or
both
([Ghana Data Protection Commission, registration guidance](https://dataprotection.org.gh/registration/)).
A concise factsheet on Ghana's regime is available via the African data protection
factsheet series
([dataprotection.africa Ghana factsheet](https://dataprotection.africa/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Ghana-Factsheet-updated-20200331.pdf)).
Ghana's registration-before-processing model is structurally the closest African analogue
to South Africa's Information Officer registration requirement, which makes the
compliance-module design pattern built for POPIA plausibly reusable — but this has not
been validated and remains Open.

## 6. Sequencing recommendation (Provisional)

Consistent with the go-to-market sequencing already identified in
`Teramind_assessment_and_build_thesis_2026-07-31.md` (BPO first, financial services
second, public sector third, continental fourth), continental expansion should follow,
not precede, a proven South African compliance-module pattern. The recommended order,
subject to revision once South African design-partner traction is confirmed:

1. **South Africa** — home market, all regulatory filings and the POPIA control map as
   the reference implementation.
2. **Nigeria** — largest BPO and financial-services market on the continent by volume,
   and the NDPC's registration model is analogous enough to South Africa's to reuse the
   compliance-module architecture with country-specific content, not a rebuild.
3. **Kenya** — smaller but well-governed regulatory regime, useful as a second proof
   point that the compliance-module pattern generalises across differing statutory detail
   (registration thresholds, cross-border transfer rules).
4. **Ghana** — comparable registration model to Ghana's West African neighbours,
   lower priority than Nigeria purely on market size.

This sequencing is a hypothesis to be tested against actual customer inbound demand, not
a committed roadmap — see `028_ROADMAP_AND_MILESTONES.md` for how (and whether) this
shows up in the v1–v2 timeline. No engineering work should begin on a country-specific
compliance module until a paying design partner or a clearly identified pipeline
opportunity exists in that country.

## 7. What does not change per country

The underlying architecture — tiered visibility, per-tenant key custody, hash-chained
evidence, the hard constraint against automated adverse decisions — is Locked globally
per `CANON.md` and does not vary by jurisdiction. What varies per country is: the specific
statutory citations in the compliance module, the registration and notification workflow
target (Information Regulator vs. NDPC vs. ODPC vs. Ghana DPC), the languages used for
transparency notices, and local payment-rail integration. This keeps the product's legal
and ethical foundation from fragmenting into inconsistent country-by-country compromises
as it expands.

## AI implementation constraints

- Do not build a country-specific compliance module (Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana) speculatively
  ahead of a confirmed customer or pipeline opportunity in that country, per section 6.
- Do not represent the South African operating-conditions requirements in section 1 as
  optional or configurable away; they are Locked per `CANON.md` section 16.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given the agent is deployed on a South African endpoint, when power is lost abruptly,
  then no telemetry corruption occurs and buffered data survives the outage.
- Given an endpoint is offline for up to 30 days, when connectivity is restored, then
  buffered telemetry uploads without data loss, subject to the documented backpressure
  policy.
- Given a transparency notice is issued to an employee, when the employee's preferred
  language is isiZulu, Afrikaans, Sesotho, or isiXhosa, then the notice must be available
  in that language, not English only.
- Given no confirmed customer or pipeline opportunity exists in Nigeria, Kenya, or Ghana,
  when a roadmap review occurs, then no country-specific compliance module for that
  country should appear as committed engineering work.
